China Solar Panel Shake-Up 2026: What New Rules Mean for 575W and 585W Panels

Updated August 28, 2026 · SolarRateHub News Desk

Claims that China has simply “banned all 575W-and-below solar panels” are too broad. The real story is more important: China has introduced new mandatory efficiency standards that will push manufacturers toward higher-efficiency modules from January 1, 2027. That could accelerate the clearance or export of older, less competitive stock.

Fact check: There is no evidence that China has issued a blanket rule saying every 575W or 585W panel is illegal. The new GB 47834-2026 standard is based on module efficiency and technology. Some common module formats below the new efficiency thresholds may become harder to produce or sell in China's domestic market after the standard takes effect.

What Actually Changed in China?

China published the national standard GB 47834-2026 on June 27, 2026. The standard covers minimum energy-efficiency values and efficiency grades for crystalline-silicon photovoltaic modules and inverters. It is scheduled to take effect nationwide on January 1, 2027.

The standard matters because it is not written simply around a panel's wattage label. It uses conversion-efficiency requirements that vary by cell technology. Reporting on the standard indicates that the minimum Grade 3 threshold corresponds to approximately 23.2% efficiency for TOPCon and HJT modules and 23.5% for BC modules under the specified test conditions.

Because common module dimensions are increasingly capable of producing 630W-plus output at these efficiency levels, the rules can accelerate the industry's move away from older lower-output designs.

So, Are 575W and 585W Panels Banned?

Not as a blanket wattage ban. This distinction is important for buyers. A 575W or 585W label by itself does not tell you whether a module meets the new standard. The relevant questions are the module's technology, efficiency, dimensions and compliance with the applicable Chinese requirements.

In practical terms, however, the new standards can still create a major commercial effect. Manufacturers preparing for the 2027 rules have an incentive to prioritize higher-efficiency products and reduce the importance of older designs that are less competitive under the new requirements.

Why Could Older Solar Panels Move to Other Countries?

China's PV industry has been dealing with enormous manufacturing capacity relative to demand. A July 2026 Canadian Border Services Agency review found that Chinese PV production capacity had exceeded 200% of global demand in 2024. The agency also noted that Chinese export volumes of wafers, cells and modules increased while their export values fell sharply, a pattern consistent with lower international prices and strong export pressure.

That does not prove that every 575W or 585W panel being sold overseas is “old stock.” It does, however, provide a credible market reason for buyers to pay attention to inventory age, manufacturing date, model generation and warranty when unusually cheap modules appear in overseas markets.

Pakistan Could See More Older-Generation Modules

Pakistan is already an important destination for Chinese solar modules. Trade records show shipments from China to Pakistan containing 575W and 585W modules. Market reports have also raised the possibility that older 585W inventory could become more available at discounted prices as manufacturers shift toward higher-efficiency products ahead of the 2027 standard.

For Pakistani buyers, a lower price can be attractive, but the key question is whether the discount reflects a genuine market opportunity or simply inventory that a supplier wants to clear.

What Should a Buyer Check Before Buying Cheap 575W or 585W Panels?

  1. Manufacturing date: Ask for the production month and year, not just the model name.
  2. Exact model number: Check the manufacturer's official datasheet.
  3. Cell technology: Confirm whether the module is TOPCon, HJT, BC or another technology.
  4. Module efficiency: Compare efficiency, not wattage alone.
  5. Serial numbers: Ask whether serial numbers can be verified with the manufacturer.
  6. Warranty: Confirm product and performance warranty terms and who will handle a claim.
  7. Physical condition: Inspect pallets, packaging, glass, frames, junction boxes and connectors.
  8. Price difference: Compare the discounted panel against a current-generation module on a per-watt and per-square-metre basis.

Why Wattage Alone Can Be Misleading

A 575W panel is not automatically worse than a 630W panel. Physical size matters. If a 575W module is smaller or has a different cell configuration, its efficiency and installation footprint may still make sense for a particular project.

Conversely, a high-wattage panel is not automatically a better buy. Buyers should compare efficiency, temperature coefficient, degradation rate, bifacial performance, warranty and physical dimensions along with the headline wattage.

What Does This Mean for Global Solar Prices?

The new Chinese standards are one part of a much larger restructuring of the global PV industry. China has enormous manufacturing capacity, while governments and companies around the world are trying to manage supply-chain risk, trade measures and falling module prices.

Canada's 2026 expiry-review determination, for example, concluded that Chinese PV modules remained at risk of continued or resumed dumping and subsidization if its trade measures were removed. This is a trade-policy finding about market conditions, not proof that any individual Chinese manufacturer is dumping a particular panel.

For buyers, the practical result is that international module prices can remain volatile. A very cheap module may be genuine competitive pricing, an inventory-clearance opportunity, or a product with specifications that need closer checking.

China's Solar Industry Is Not Simply “Dumping Old Panels”

The phrase “China is dumping all its old panels overseas” is too simplistic. China's PV industry is simultaneously expanding new technologies, restructuring capacity, dealing with oversupply and responding to changing efficiency requirements.

The more defensible conclusion is that new standards and excess manufacturing capacity can increase pressure to move older or less competitive inventory into export channels. Whether a particular shipment is old stock must be verified from its model, manufacturing date and documentation.

Bottom Line

China's 2026 PV efficiency standards are a significant development for the global solar supply chain, but it is inaccurate to describe them as a simple ban on every 575W or 585W panel. The standards are based on efficiency and technology and are scheduled to take effect in China on January 1, 2027.

As manufacturers move toward higher-efficiency modules, older designs may face greater inventory and export pressure. That could create discounted opportunities in markets such as Pakistan, but buyers should verify manufacturing date, model, efficiency, serial number and warranty before treating a cheap panel as a bargain.
