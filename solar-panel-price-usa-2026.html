Solar Panel Price in USA: What Is the Average in 2026?

For a typical home, the total installed solar price is much higher than the hardware-only price of the panels. EnergySage reports an average 12 kW residential installation cost of about $31,135 before available incentives, with a low-end figure around $27,526 and a high-end figure around $33,869 in its marketplace data. The same source reports an average price of about $2.60/W before incentives. citeturn2view0

That does not mean every U.S. homeowner should expect exactly $31,135. Roof design, system size, local labor, permitting, equipment selection, financing and utility rules can move the final quote substantially.

Panel-Only Price vs. Full Installed Solar Price

This is one of the most important differences for buyers. A panel advertised at a low dollar-per-watt price is only one part of the project. EnergySage's 2026 cost breakdown puts solar panels at about 12% of a typical installation's total cost, with inverters, wiring, racking, permitting, installation, overhead and other costs making up the rest. citeturn2view0

Wholesale equipment can be much cheaper than a finished residential installation. For example, the Q3 2026 PES Solar Equipment Price Index reported a median in-stock wholesale panel price of about $0.27/W across more than 4,300 analyzable panel SKUs. That is an equipment-market benchmark, not a homeowner-installed price. citeturn0search2

What Changes the Price of Solar in the USA?

  • System size: A larger home or higher electricity consumption usually requires more kW.
  • Roof complexity: Multiple roof planes, steep pitches, skylights and shading can increase labor and equipment requirements.
  • Local labor and permitting: Installer costs vary considerably between markets.
  • Panel technology: Higher-efficiency and premium equipment can cost more but may make sense where roof space is limited.
  • Inverter choice: String inverters, hybrid systems, optimizers and microinverters have different costs and design advantages.
  • Battery storage: Adding storage can substantially increase the project price but may provide backup power and greater energy flexibility.
  • Utility rules: Net-metering and other local programs can change the financial value of a solar system.

What About the Federal Solar Tax Credit in 2026?

Homeowners should be careful with older articles that still advertise a blanket 30% federal residential solar tax credit for new 2026 installations. The IRS states that the Residential Clean Energy Credit applies to qualified property installed through December 31, 2025 and is not available for property placed in service after that date. citeturn1search0turn1search3

State, local, utility and other programs can still affect the economics of a project, but eligibility varies. Check current government and utility information for your location before using an incentive in your budget.

How to Compare Two Solar Quotes

  • Compare the total system size in kW, not only the number of panels.
  • Check the panel model, wattage and product warranty.
  • Check the inverter model and warranty separately.
  • Ask whether racking, wiring, permits and interconnection are included.
  • Confirm whether the quote includes a battery or is solar-only.
  • Ask for the estimated annual production in kWh.
  • Compare cash price and financing separately so interest does not hide the real project cost.
  • Get more than one written quote when possible.

Bottom Line: Is Solar Expensive in the USA in 2026?

Solar is still a major home investment, but the price is highly location-dependent. Current 2026 marketplace data puts many residential projects in the broad range of roughly $2–$3+ per watt, with a typical 12 kW project around $31,135 before incentives in EnergySage's marketplace data. State averages can be lower or higher, and the final quote depends on the property and equipment. citeturn2view0

The smartest approach is to compare complete installed quotes rather than chasing the cheapest panel price. Look at system output, equipment warranties, installer reputation, financing terms and the rules of your local utility before deciding.
