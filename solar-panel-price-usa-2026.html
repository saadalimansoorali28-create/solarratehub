Solar Panel Price in USA: What Is the Average in 2026?

For a typical home, the total installed solar price is much higher than the hardware-only price of the panels. EnergySage's marketplace data shows an average 12 kW residential installation around $31,135 before incentives, with actual projects varying by location, roof, equipment and installer.

That does not mean every U.S. homeowner should expect exactly $31,135. Roof design, system size, local labor, permitting, equipment selection, financing and utility rules can move the final quote substantially.

Panel-Only Price vs. Full Installed Solar Price

A panel advertised at a low dollar-per-watt price is only one part of the project. A residential quote also covers inverter equipment, racking, wiring, labor, permits, design, inspections and other project costs.

Wholesale equipment can be much cheaper than a finished residential installation. The Q3 2026 PES Solar Equipment Price Index reported a median in-stock wholesale panel price of about $0.27/W. That is an equipment-market benchmark, not a homeowner-installed price. View the PES index.

What Changes the Price of Solar in the USA?

  • System size: Higher electricity consumption usually requires more kW.
  • Roof complexity: Multiple roof planes, steep pitches and shading can increase labor.
  • Local labor and permitting: Installer costs vary considerably between markets.
  • Panel technology: Higher-efficiency equipment can cost more but may help where roof space is limited.
  • Inverter choice: String inverters, optimizers and microinverters have different costs and design advantages.
  • Battery storage: Storage can add substantially to project cost while providing backup and energy flexibility.
  • Utility rules: Local export and interconnection rules can affect the financial value of solar.

Federal Incentives: Check the Current Rules

Older solar articles often quote incentive rules that may no longer apply to a 2026 project. Before including any federal, state or utility incentive in your budget, verify the current eligibility rules with the official government or utility source for your location.

How to Compare Two Solar Quotes

  • Compare total system size in kW, not only panel count.
  • Check panel model, wattage and product warranty.
  • Check the inverter model and warranty separately.
  • Ask whether racking, wiring, permits and interconnection are included.
  • Confirm whether the quote includes a battery.
  • Ask for estimated annual production in kWh.
  • Compare cash price and financing separately.
  • Get more than one written quote when possible.

Bottom Line: Is Solar Expensive in the USA in 2026?

Solar remains a major home investment, but the price is highly location-dependent. Current 2026 marketplace benchmarks put many residential projects in the broad $2–$3+ per watt range, with a typical 12 kW project around the low-$30,000 range before incentives. The right quote depends on the property, system design and equipment.

The smartest approach is to compare complete installed quotes rather than chasing the cheapest panel price. Look at system output, equipment warranties, installer reputation, financing terms and your local utility rules.
