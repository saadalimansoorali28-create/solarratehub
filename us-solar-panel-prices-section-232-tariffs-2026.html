Why Are U.S. Solar Panel Prices Rising in September 2026? Section 232 Tariffs Explained

Updated September 5, 2026

U.S. SOLAR PRICE PRESSURE15% duty · $0.38/W module MIP · Dec. 4, 2026

U.S. solar buyers are entering a more complicated pricing environment in late 2026. New Section 232 trade measures on polysilicon and its derivatives introduce minimum import prices and an additional duty on covered imports. Solar market analysts say the measures have already pushed module pricing higher, even though the main new import-price rules take effect on December 4, 2026.

The policy is important for homeowners because solar modules are only one part of an installed system, but a change in module pricing can still affect installer quotes, procurement timing and the economics of larger projects.

What Changed With Section 232?

On August 6, 2026, the White House issued a proclamation covering polysilicon and polysilicon derivative products used in the solar and semiconductor supply chains. The action establishes minimum import prices and a 15% ad valorem duty on covered polysilicon derivatives, with the relevant provisions taking effect for goods entered for consumption or withdrawn from warehouse for consumption on or after December 4, 2026.

For solar, the headline numbers are a $0.22 per watt minimum import price for solar cells and a $0.38 per watt minimum import price for solar modules. The proclamation also establishes minimum prices for polysilicon and ingots/wafers. White House proclamation

Important distinction: The $0.38/W figure is a minimum import-price rule for covered solar modules. It is not the retail price a homeowner will necessarily pay for a finished panel or installed solar system.

The White House says the additional duties apply in addition to other applicable duties and charges except where the proclamation provides otherwise. It also sets special treatment for certain products from Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Switzerland, Liechtenstein and the European Union, and a separate 10% additional rate for covered products from the United Kingdom. Country-of-origin and tariff treatment therefore need to be checked for the actual equipment being procured.

Why Are Module Prices Rising Before December 4?

The market can react before a formal effective date. Developers, distributors and manufacturers make procurement decisions based on expected future costs, inventory availability and contract timing.

pv magazine reported on September 1 that Section 232 tariffs and minimum import prices had pushed U.S. solar module spot prices above 40 cents per watt. Intertek CEA analysts cited in the report said the combination of the 38-cent module MIP, the 15% ad valorem duty and other applicable tariffs had pushed spot pricing for imported and domestic modules to roughly 46 cents per watt. pv magazine analysis

That does not mean every panel quote in America is suddenly 46 cents per watt. Spot pricing is a market indicator, while an installer quote includes procurement, logistics, design, labor, permitting, overhead, inverter, mounting, electrical work and other project costs.

What Could Higher Module Prices Mean for Homeowners?

The biggest effect is likely to be on the equipment portion of a quote rather than a direct one-for-one increase in the total installed price. For example, if a 10kW project's module cost moved from a hypothetical $0.30/W to $0.46/W, the module-only difference would be about $1,600. That is not the same as saying the homeowner's final installed price will rise by exactly $1,600.

What Does the $0.38/W Module Floor Look Like?

If a covered imported module is subject to the $0.38/W minimum import price, the floor itself corresponds to:

  • 5kW of modules: $1,900 at $0.38/W
  • 10kW of modules: $3,800 at $0.38/W
  • 12kW of modules: $4,560 at $0.38/W
  • 15kW of modules: $5,700 at $0.38/W

Again, this is a policy benchmark, not a consumer invoice. A homeowner also pays for inverters, mounting, electrical equipment, labor, permitting, design, logistics and potentially storage.

What Happens on December 4, 2026?

The proclamation says the minimum import price program and specified duties apply to covered goods entered for consumption, or withdrawn from warehouse for consumption, on or after 12:01 a.m. Eastern Time on December 4, 2026. Existing contracts can have special treatment in some circumstances, and country-specific provisions can change the exact tariff calculation.

For a homeowner, the practical takeaway is not simply “buy before December 4.” It is to ask the installer where the equipment is coming from, whether the quoted price is locked, how long the quote is valid, and whether tariff or procurement adjustments are allowed in the contract.

Should You Buy Solar Before December 2026?

There is no universal deadline strategy. Buying quickly can make sense when you already have a strong quote, your project is ready and the contract protects you from unexpected equipment-price changes. Waiting can make sense when the roof, financing or system design is not ready.

Do not sign a solar contract only because of the tariff deadline. Compare at least two or three complete quotes, check the equipment model numbers, confirm the warranty, review escalation clauses and compare the total installed price—not just the panel price.

Questions to ask your installer

  • What exact panel model and wattage are included?
  • Are the panels already in U.S. inventory?
  • Is the quoted equipment price locked through installation?
  • Can tariff or procurement costs be passed through to the customer?
  • Where were the modules and cells manufactured?
  • What inverter, mounting and battery equipment is included?
  • Is the quote cash, loan, lease or PPA pricing?
  • How long is the quote valid?

FAQ

Is there a $0.38 per watt solar panel price in the USA?

No. $0.38/W is the minimum import price set for covered solar modules under the 2026 Section 232 proclamation. It is not a universal retail or installed price.

When do the new Section 232 solar rules take effect?

The proclamation specifies December 4, 2026 at 12:01 a.m. Eastern Time for the covered imports and duties described in the order.

Will every U.S. solar panel cost more?

Not necessarily. Exposure depends on supply chain, country of origin, inventory, contract timing, domestic assembly and the exact tariff treatment of the product.

Will a 10kW solar system cost $1,600 more?

Not automatically. $1,600 is only an illustration of the module-only difference if a hypothetical 10kW module package moved from $0.30/W to $0.46/W. Installed project pricing includes many other costs.

Why are solar prices moving before December 4?

Markets can price expected future costs before a rule becomes effective. Procurement contracts, inventory decisions and distributor pricing can move ahead of the formal date.
