China Solar Panel Price in August 2026: TOPCon Module Rates

China remains one of the world's most important solar manufacturing hubs. In August 2026, module prices are being shaped by production costs, technology, export demand, inventory and regional market conditions. Here is a practical snapshot of the latest available pricing signals.

Updated: 28 August 2026 · SolarRateHub Market Overview

Quick answer: Recent 2026 industry data put new ground-mounted TOPCon module contracts in China around RMB 0.71–0.75 per watt, while reported FOB prices outside China were around US$0.115–0.125/W. These are industry price indicators, not fixed retail prices for every module or buyer.

China Solar Panel Price Today: What the Market Shows

There is no single nationwide retail price for a solar panel in China. The number a buyer sees depends on module power, cell technology, factory, order size, destination, payment terms, shipping and whether the quoted figure is a domestic price, FOB price or a delivered price.

InfoLink's 2026 SNEC PV+ market report said new contracts for ground-mounted TOPCon modules in China were being signed around RMB 0.71–0.75/W, while FOB prices outside China were around US$0.115–0.125/W. The same report noted that higher-output TOPCon 3.0 and BC products could command premiums.

What Does That Mean for a 580W or 600W Panel?

A per-watt benchmark can be used for a simple planning calculation, but it should not be confused with a final invoice price. For example, applying the RMB 0.71–0.75/W TOPCon contract range to a theoretical 580W module gives about RMB 412–435 before considering packaging, logistics, taxes, margins, certification, installation or other commercial costs.

For a theoretical 600W module, the same calculation gives about RMB 426–450. These are mathematical illustrations based on a per-watt benchmark, not quoted prices for a particular brand or model.

China Solar Module Exports in July 2026

Recent SMM data provide another useful view of the market. China exported an estimated 16.45 GW of solar modules in July 2026, down about 14.96% from 19.34 GW in June and about 21.27% year over year.

The reported July export value was approximately US$1.90 billion. Dividing the export value by the estimated shipment volume produces an implied blended export value of about US$0.116/W. SMM specifically describes this as a combined customs-value indicator, so it should not be treated as the transaction price of a particular TOPCon, BC, HJT or other module.

Important: A fall in monthly exports does not automatically mean that Chinese solar panels have become cheaper. Shipment timing, inventory adjustments and changes in individual destination markets can move export volumes without representing a simple change in factory pricing.

Why China Solar Panel Prices Change

  • Polysilicon and wafer costs: Upstream material prices affect the economics of cells and modules.
  • Cell technology: TOPCon, BC, HJT and other technologies can carry different costs and premiums.
  • Factory utilization: Capacity, production schedules and competition affect offers.
  • Export demand: Large destination markets can change shipment volumes and supplier pricing.
  • Inventory: Existing stock may be priced differently from newly manufactured modules.
  • Shipping and trade costs: Freight, insurance, tariffs, duties and local compliance can materially change delivered cost.
  • Order size: Container-scale or project-scale purchases generally do not compare directly with small retail purchases.

China Solar Panel Price vs. Delivered Price

One of the most common mistakes when comparing Chinese solar prices is comparing a factory or FOB number with the final price paid by a homeowner or installer. A low per-watt factory benchmark does not include every cost between the manufacturing plant and the final project.

  • Confirm the exact module model and wattage.
  • Ask whether the price is EXW, FOB, CIF or another Incoterm.
  • Check shipping, insurance and port charges.
  • Check applicable duties, tariffs and taxes in the destination country.
  • Include certification and compliance requirements where applicable.
  • Compare warranty terms and supplier reputation.
  • For project purchases, compare the complete delivered cost per watt.

What China Prices Mean for Global Solar Buyers

China's module market matters far beyond China because Chinese manufacturers supply a large share of the global photovoltaic value chain. Changes in Chinese module costs can influence procurement decisions in Europe, Asia, the Middle East, Africa and other markets.

For US buyers, however, a Chinese module benchmark should not be used as a direct prediction of the installed price of a solar system in the United States. US pricing also depends on domestic supply chains, trade policy, logistics, installer costs, financing and project-specific requirements.
