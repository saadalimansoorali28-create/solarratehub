What does “panel-only price” mean?

It means the comparison is limited to the photovoltaic module. It does not represent the price of a complete residential solar installation.

Installation, inverter, battery storage, racking, wiring, labor, permits, taxes, shipping and other project costs are excluded. A homeowner should therefore never treat the figures above as a complete-system quote.

Why compare solar panels by $/kW?

Panel wattages are not identical. One module might produce around 430 W while another is 580 W or more. Looking only at the price of one physical panel can therefore give the wrong impression.

Converting the number to dollars per kilowatt creates a simpler apples-to-apples measure. For example, $0.45/W × 1,000 = $450/kW.

This is especially useful when reviewing distributor lists or comparing quotes that use different panel models.

How to interpret the September 1–14, 2026 market

Trina, Jinko and Canadian Solar

The selected examples for these three brands are tightly grouped around $450–$470/kW. Small differences can easily move as inventory, order size and supplier pricing change.

REC

The selected REC Alpha Pure-RX example is higher at about $670/kW. A premium module can command a higher price, so panel efficiency, warranty and degradation should also be considered rather than focusing on price alone.

Qcells

The $880/kW figure is a domestic-content example. It should not be interpreted as the universal price of every Qcells module available in the U.S. market.

What can make the price change?

  • Panel model: wattage, efficiency and technology can change the module price.
  • Order quantity: pallet and larger-volume purchases can have different pricing.
  • Domestic-content status: qualifying products can price differently from standard imports.
  • Supplier and location: distributor inventory and regional logistics affect the number.
  • Delivery: freight and availability can change the effective cost.

The figures in this article are for market comparison and planning. Always confirm the current written price with the supplier before purchase.

Bottom line

For the September 1–14, 2026 comparison, the selected U.S. panel-only examples range from about $450/kW to $880/kW. The lower-priced examples are clustered around $450–$470/kW, while the selected REC and Qcells domestic-content examples sit higher.

If you are comparing a solar quote, ask for the module cost to be shown separately from the inverter, battery, racking, labor and other project costs. That gives you a much clearer picture of what you are actually paying for the panels.
